title: Resilience and Resourcefulness

---
For several years now I have been talking about 𝗥&𝗥. No, not really Rest and Recreation (although I could really do with some 😜) but rather 𝗥𝗲𝘀𝗶𝗹𝗶𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲 and 𝗥𝗲𝘀𝗼𝘂𝗿𝗰𝗲𝗳𝘂𝗹𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀.\
\
Increasingly, I see these not just as useful traits, but as two key “muscles” we need to keep exercising. Like any muscle, they weaken when neglected, strengthen when tested, and become more instinctive when we practise them deliberately.\
\
**[\#𝗥𝗲𝘀𝗶𝗹𝗶𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23resilience&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED)** is what allows us to hang on and #𝗽𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿𝗲 despite the obstacles and challenges that we are facing. It is the strength of body, mind and soul that helps us absorb setbacks, regulate our responses, and continue moving forward even when the path is painful or unclear. Reading **[Angela Duckworth](https://www.linkedin.com/in/angeladuckworth/)**'s 𝑮𝑹𝑰𝑻 gives a very good sense of how passion and perseverance can mean the difference between those who can — and those who caved.\
\
But resilience on its own is not always enough. If resilience keeps us in the fight and **[\#𝗥𝗲𝘀𝗼𝘂𝗿𝗰𝗲𝗳𝘂𝗹𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23resourcefulness&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED)** helps us win it. It is the ability to ask better questions, reframe constraints, find alternatives, activate networks, combine ideas, and draw on both 𝒊𝒏𝒕𝒆𝒓𝒏𝒂𝒍 strengths and 𝒆𝒙𝒕𝒆𝒓𝒏𝒂𝒍 support. Lessons in resourcefulness are not always obvious; they require self-awareness, agility, curiosity, humility, and the willingness to admit that the answer may not already be within us.\
\
In that sense, the two work differently. Resilience is often about endurance: “Can I keep going?” Resourcefulness is often about adaptation: “Is there another way?” Resilience gives us the staying power to withstand pressure; resourcefulness gives us the creativity and practical intelligence to change the equation. One is about not giving up too quickly. The other is about not being trapped by the first solution, the most familiar route, or the limits of our own current perspective.\
\
We need both because life rarely presents challenges that are solved by strength alone or by cleverness alone. When we face rejection, illness, career disruption, family pressures, financial strain, leadership uncertainty, or simply the fatigue of daily responsibilities, resilience keeps us from collapsing under the weight of the situation. Resourcefulness then helps us identify what can be learned, who can help, what can be changed, and which next step is still available.\
\
Without resilience, resourcefulness can become restless improvisation — lots of options, but not enough resolve to see any of them through. Without resourcefulness, resilience can become stubborn endurance — admirable strength, but sometimes applied to a path that may no longer be wise, effective, or sustainable. Together, they create a more complete response to adversity: the courage to stay the course when needed, and the wisdom to change course when necessary.\
\
Life holds many lessons on why and how we can become more resilient, from picking ourselves up when we fall learning to walk as toddlers, to bouncing back when life puts us down. But resourcefulness often has to be cultivated more intentionally: by widening our exposure, building relationships before we need them, learning across disciplines, asking for help early, and training ourselves to see constraints not only as barriers but also as prompts for creativity.\
\
Being Resilient helps us to stay in the fight, but being Resourceful often determines how the fight is won. And just like our other muscles, the more we exercise 𝗕𝗢𝗧𝗛 our resilience and resourcefulness 💪, the stronger they become — and the more natural our reflexes when life inevitably tests us again.
